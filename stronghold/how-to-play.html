How a turn works

Read the night. Give the orders. Submit once.

A turn is not a session you play through — it is a morning at a desk. Most take five minutes. A turn in the middle of a war takes longer, because more people want something.

1 · Read what happened overnight

You arrive to a day already in progress. The Chronicle has the night's record, the Mail has your correspondence, the Dispatches carry what couriers heard about other people's quarrels, and the map notes what changed shape. On an ordinary day that is fifteen to twenty new things, none of them invented — every one is anchored to something the simulation actually resolved.

Your council opens with a judgement rather than a figure. The steward on a thin granary, the marshal on a border, the chancellor on what the month cost.

2 · Give the day's orders

Orders are staged, not executed. You move between rooms setting whatever the day calls for — taxes, building, the levy, a column, an embassy — and nothing happens to any of it until you submit.

Nothing is lost while you do this. The staged day lives with your campaign on the server, not in the page, so you can set half of it on your phone at breakfast, close it, and finish on a different machine at lunch. The orders will be exactly where you left them.

3 · Submit

Submitting resolves the day for the whole world at once. Every AI lord's orders go through the same validation gate yours do — none of them can spend coin they lack or field troops they do not have — and every hostile action reads a snapshot taken before any of them apply. Simultaneous means simultaneous. Nobody moves second.

Then it is done. The orders cannot be recalled, and the next day opens tomorrow.

Missed daysnever replay — there is no catching up
The clockis the server's, so changing your device's date does nothing

A campaign is around a hundred and fifty committed days. Nothing you do in one of them decides it, and nothing you fail at ends it: from a wrecked province with no civilians, unpayable troops and an empty treasury, a player who starts governing sensibly is back to over a thousand acres and twenty thousand people inside a year.

The rooms open as you earn them

You do not begin with all fifteen. Four arrive on milestones you reach by playing, and the day each typically lands on is a consequence rather than a timer.

The Court opens inside the Kingdom room around day twenty; great works appear in the Province room around day forty-five.

Winning

Your kingdom holds every acre in the world, and holds it for ten more days. Reaching that means rising from Lord to Crown — forty-five points of standing to become a Councilor, a hundred and twenty for Regent, two hundred and forty for the crown itself — and then bringing eleven other realms in, by siege or by treaty.

You can never march on your own realm. Its provinces come with you when you take the throne.
