# Sky finder object facts

`sky-object-facts.json` contains 42 compact entries: 20 named stars, 13 constellations, the seven other major planets, Sun and Moon. Keys match the common names in the chart, including `Ursa Major` and `Ursa Minor`. Earth is the observing location, not an object in Earth's sky.

Each entry has `summary`, `findTip`, `sourceUrl` and `sourceName`. `findTipSourceUrl` and `findTipSourceName` optionally record a separate observing reference. Facts are paraphrases of the linked primary sources: NASA and NASA/JPL, ESO, PAGASA and the University of Arizona's Flandrau Science Center & Planetarium. No source contributes more than 200 words. No images are copied.

Use the calculated chart for current altitude, azimuth, rise/set, brightness and visibility state. These static notes do not assert that a body is visible now. Seasonal source pages inform stable patterns only; their dated planet placements are not imported. Distances are omitted where authoritative references differ materially (notably Deneb). Star colors are qualitative, not a guarantee of visible color under every condition.

The notes should be available when selecting an object, not presented as a large mandatory explanatory menu. Preserve the named source link beside the facts. No daily refresh is required for this static educational text. Revisit only for an explicit correction, relevant source change or observed broken link.

NASA's skywatching FAQ also gives approximate angular orientation aids: a fist held at arm's length spans roughly 10 degrees, three middle fingers about 5 degrees, and a little finger about 1 degree: https://science.nasa.gov/skywatching/faq/ . These are approximate human guides, not calibrated measurements.
