Sky chart sources

Sky right now calculates positions on your device for the selected location and current time. It is a chart, not a camera view or a measurement of local visibility.

Positions

Astronomy Engine 2.1.19, © 2019–2023 Don Cross, MIT license. The bundled source retains its license. It supplies topocentric planet, Sun and Moon positions, precession/nutation, horizon coordinates, refraction and rise/set calculations.

Stars and constellations

921 stars through catalog magnitude 4.5 and the figures of all 88 constellations, with Serpens displayed in its two parts, are adapted from d3-celestial by Olaf Frohn. Star data originate in the XHIP Extended Hipparcos Compilation (Anderson & Francis, 2012); star-name indexes and constellation figure sources are documented in the project's readme. J2000 coordinates are transformed to the observing date. Star proper motion is not applied; this is a visual finding chart, not astrometry. Variable-star catalog magnitudes are not current brightness measurements.

d3-celestial © 2015 Olaf Frohn, BSD 3-Clause license. Changes: selected stars through magnitude 4.5, compact data fields, English/common names, separate identifiers for Serpens Caput and Cauda, and local horizon projection. Constellation figures are illustrative patterns rather than official region boundaries.

Learning and finding

Object descriptions link to NASA/JPL, ESO, PAGASA and Flandrau sources directly in each object's details. NASA's skywatching FAQ describes the roughly 10-degree fist-at-arm's-length guide. Actual visibility depends on daylight, clouds, light pollution, eyesight and local obstructions.

Manual chart bearings use true north. A phone compass may use magnetic north and may be affected by metal or calibration; it is an approximate aid. Location permission is requested only when you press “Use my location”; calculations stay on the device.
